Emory Douglas is a designer and artist whose work is deeply rooted in community, empowerment, and social justice. Growing up in Grand Rapids in 1943 and raised in San Francisco, he discovered printing and design early on – beginning in a juvenile detention center’s print shop and later while studying graphic design in college. Later on using those tools to create powerful visuals as the “Revolutionary Artist” and Minister in Culture for the Black Panther Party during the height of political tension in the 1960s and 70s. His bold posters and newspaper layouts spoke directly to Black communities, offering both critique and hope, while giving people a clear, unapologetic visual language for their experiences. By treating design as a tool for communication and collective resilience, Douglas helped shape a movement and showed how imagery can uplift voices that are too often overlooked. Continuing to inspire designers using visual art as a catalyst for activism today.
